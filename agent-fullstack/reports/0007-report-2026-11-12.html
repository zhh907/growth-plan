1 · CONTEXT ECONOMICS: A SLICED FILE, A HALF-PRICE BILL
One whole-file audit of paper A cost 3150 input tokens; sliced into three segments with three auditors, the total came to 1472 — half price. Same logic as your 700-line EA audit costing 206K: the bill follows "how much each model must look at". Slicing is divide-and-conquer for context — what you save is not just time, but real tokens.
And each small-scope auditor keeps a tighter mouth: what it cannot see, it marks NOT JUDGEABLE — never invented. Small view, no filler.
2 · 判卷标准(rubric):歧义是会被放大的
rubric = 判卷标准,就是提示词里"什么算违规"的定义。今天它第二次咬人:checklist 写"no check for already-open orders",整文件审计员读成语义版(MaxTrades=5 不算拒绝),段审计员读成字面版(有上限就算有检查)——同一份代码,两种判罚。
并联放大了这个伤害,因为三个独立上下文没有共享的判案先例。修法你已经会了:把"有没有检查"这种可以多种解读的问题,改写成"同类持仓已存在时,新信号还会不会成交"——只有一个答案的问题。
和你写 EA 一个道理:开仓条件永远写成可判定的表达式,不写"趋势看起来不错"。
2 · THE RUBRIC: AMBIGUITY GETS AMPLIFIED
A rubric is the grading standard — the checklist's definition of "what counts as a violation". Today it bit for the second time: the checklist said "no check for already-open orders". The whole-file auditor read it semantically (a MaxTrades of 5 is no refusal); the segment auditor read it literally (a cap exists, so a check exists) — same code, opposite verdicts.
Parallelism amplifies this, because three independent contexts share no case law. The fix you already know: turn "is there a check" (multiple readings) into "can a new signal still fire while a same-type position is open" (one answer).
Same discipline as your EAs: entry conditions are decidable expressions, never "the trend looks decent".

Today's most expensive lesson. SYMPTOM: THE GUARD EXISTS, YET "NO GUARD" WAS FILED
Paper C sliced into three segments (1-25 / 26-50 / 51-75). Ground truth: exactly one seed, SL=0 at line 57.
Result: segment 3 caught the SL ✅ (1/1 hits); but segment 2 filed "SAME-BAR [HIGH]", citing the signal at 28-31 and the MaxTrades cap at 47-49 — on the grounds that "no guard exists in the visible path". The root cause is not dishonesty — the facts needed for the judgment were split across two slices, and neither was complete. Lesson: parallel's savings are real, and so is its blindness; book both.

① RUBRIC
The word you asked about yourself. /ˈruːbrɪk/, from Latin ruber (red) — medieval monks wrote chapter instructions in red ink, and the red letters became "the standard you follow".
Frequent pairs: grading rubric; tighten the rubric — your most-repeated action these two days. Same root family: ruby, rubella ("little red" disease).
② fan-out / fan-in — 扇出 / 扇入
今天代码的主角。fan-out = 任务从一点散出去给多个工人;fan-in = 结果从多处收拢回一点。像折扇:从扇轴散开是 out,收拢回来是 in。
用法:可作名词(a fan-out of 3 auditors)也可作动词(to fan out the work)。硬件圈的老词——一个输出接多个负载就叫 fan-out,软件圈借来描述任务分发。
② FAN-OUT / FAN-IN
Today's headline pair. Fan-out = tasks scattering from one point to many workers; fan-in = results converging from many back to one. Like a folding fan: opening from the pivot is out, closing back is in.
Usage: works as a noun ("a fan-out of 3 auditors") and as a verb ("to fan out the work"). Old hardware term — one output driving many loads was called fan-out; software borrowed it for task distribution.
③ wall-clock — 墙钟时间
程序输出里的 wall-clock=61.9s:你手表真实走过的时间。它和"CPU 时间"相对——三个 agent 等 API 回包时,CPU 几乎在睡觉,但墙上的钟照走。并联优化的就是墙钟:61.9s ≈ 最慢的一段,而不是三段之和。
记法:clock 挂在墙上,对所有人都一样公平地走——真实流逝的时间。
③ WALL-CLOCK
From today's output: wall-clock=61.9s — the time your watch actually measured. Contrast with "CPU time": while three agents wait for API replies, the CPU mostly sleeps, but the clock on the wall keeps walking. Parallelism optimizes wall-clock: 61.9s ≈ the slowest segment, not the sum of three.
Memory hook: the clock on the wall ticks the same for everyone — real elapsed time.
④ judgeable — 可判定的
段审计员的口头禅 NOT JUDGEABLE FROM SEGMENT:仅凭我这一段,判不了。judge(判断)+ able(能够)——注意不是 judgable,judge 结尾的 e 保留后再加 -able。
这个词今天立了大功:三段共 6 次 NOT JUDGEABLE,零编造。诚实的"我不知道",比自信的瞎猜值钱得多——这条对模型和人一样成立。
④ JUDGEABLE
The segment auditors' refrain: NOT JUDGEABLE FROM SEGMENT — "from my slice alone, I cannot rule". Judge + able — note the spelling: judgeable, the e survives before -able.
This word earned its keep today: six NOT-JUDGEABLE verdicts across three segments, zero fabrication. An honest "I cannot tell" beats a confident guess — true for models and for people alike.
